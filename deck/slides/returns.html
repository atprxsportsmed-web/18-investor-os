These are cash-only returns: no sale value and no returned capital are counted, so they are deliberately conservative. On dues-only revenue with 5% churn per quarter, the Base case repays the investor in Year 4. Higher churn lowers profit and delays payback, so retention is the main lever to discuss. The owner forgoing distributions until payback is the main strength of the structure.
